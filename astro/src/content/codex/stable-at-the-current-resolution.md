# Stable at the Current Resolution

![A studio workbench holds a faint graphite study, a fragile paper-and-wire model, and a more resolved architectural form.](</media/codex/stable-at-the-current-resolution/stable-at-the-current-resolution.webp>)

The design is never done.

It arrives at states.

That changes the ontology of *finished*.

A design is not an object moving cleanly from unfinished to complete. It is an evolving thing arriving at a state sufficiently coherent for the present conditions.

Those conditions include time, budget, knowledge, technology, audience, organizational appetite, implementation capacity, and what the thing presently needs to accomplish.

So the sequence looks less like this:

> unfinished → finished

And more like this:

> coherent enough to examine  
> coherent enough to choose  
> coherent enough to build  
> coherent enough to release  
> coherent enough to learn from  
> revised by what we learned

The useful word may be **resolution**.

Not resolution merely as visual fidelity. Resolution of uncertainty.

Each pass resolves some combination of form, behavior, hierarchy, content, technical feasibility, stakeholder agreement, edge cases, and implementation detail.

Something can be sufficiently resolved to discuss but not to approve. Sufficiently resolved to approve but not to build. Sufficiently resolved to build but not yet sufficiently resolved to scale.

Resolution is purpose-relative.

This makes time and budget easier to speak about honestly. They determine how much resolution can be purchased now. They do not magically turn an inhabited, changing system into a finished object.

The better question is not:

**Can we finish it?**

It is:

**What resolution does this need to reach before we can responsibly put it down?**

Then clean the workbench.

Consolidate the current state. Resolve what would be irresponsible to leave dangling. Record what remains open. Make the next move legible. Put the thing on the shelf without making it haunt everybody.

Later, when conditions change, it becomes material again.

This feels especially true of digital design. Websites are not marble statues waiting beneath a sheet. They are inhabited systems. Content changes. People behave unexpectedly. Organizations shift. Technologies move. Business priorities wander into the room carrying coffee.

The craft is not completion.

It is **state stewardship**: knowing how to enter a changing thing, increase its resolution, stabilize it for its present purpose, and leave enough intelligence behind for the next return.

A design can be stable at its current resolution without being finished.

That is not failure to complete the work.

That is a more truthful description of the work.
